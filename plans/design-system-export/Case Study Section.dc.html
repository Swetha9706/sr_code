01 Method

Surveys and interviews

Understand user behaviours, attitudes, and pain points around retirement planning.

Survey execution

{{ s.n }} {{ s.label }}

12 participants were recruited through Reddit and Discord. 11 were based in the UK. 35% were aged 25–30 and 63% were between 35 and 45. The sample was heavily male (90%), and nearly half worked in tech.

This group had above-average financial literacy. All of them still avoided retirement planning and felt anxious about it. That ruled out a simple knowledge gap.

Expected retirement duration
Under 30 10–15 years
Over 30 15–20 years

The only measurable age difference was expected retirement duration: under-30s anticipated 10–15 years, over-30s expected 15–20, likely because younger respondents face higher living costs and fewer guaranteed income-earning years. In all other respects, confusion was consistent across ages. Around 50% said retirement did not factor into major purchasing decisions. When asked what a typical day in retirement might look like, participants described travel, gardening, and pottery — but had no savings strategy aligned with any of it.

Interview execution

Four in-depth interviews with individuals aged 27–42. Both methods used a Buzzfeed-style quiz as a boundary object — drawn from Frith et al. (2005) and Bagnoli (2009) — with unlabelled imagery so participants could describe their future without being anchored by category labels.

Reference

McGonigal (2017) — when asked to think about their distant future, people neurologically envision someone else, making choices for that self harder. This grounded the decision to design toward imagination rather than information.

The Core Insight

Regardless of age, people are aimless about retirement planning not because they lack information, but because they lack imagination about their future selves.

Most tools count. None help you imagine.
